Satellite georeferencing needs reference imagery to match against, and the standard
instrument is a database of ground-control-point chips cut from imagery whose geometry is
trusted. Such imagery is often licensed, and the chips cannot be shared. Generated
references were proposed to sidestep that: render a Sentinel-2-like image from freely
available vector and land-cover data, and match the real scene against the rendering.

GenCP \cite{guasch2026gencp} is the instance studied here. A conditional GAN
\cite{isola2017image} renders a Sentinel-2-like image from OpenStreetMap vectors and a
land-cover raster. Its published objective for the 10~m model is adversarial +
$\lambda\cdot$LPIPS \cite{zhang2018lpips} with $\lambda = 100$ and a binary
cross-entropy discriminator; the upstream text states that the perceptual loss replaced
L1 to improve performance, and gives no comparison between the two and no ablation of the
adversarial term.

Two sentences of scope. At 10~m the premise for a generated reference does not bind: over
24 stratified Turkish extents, none lacked a usable Sentinel-2 scene within a year, and the
median extent had a cloud-free scene from two days before, the worst from seventeen.\footnote{Scene-per-year
counts are lower bounds, since the archive query capped at 100 and every extent hit the
cap; the gap rate and the freshness figures depend only on the most recent usable scene
and are unaffected.} Nor does currency help: on a rapidly built-up site, a five-year-old
real scene registered a 2026 target as well on the tiles that changed most as on those
that changed least, and the registered advantage for a synthetic reference built from
current OpenStreetMap was absent, $+0.008 \pm 0.031$~px.\footnote{This null is equally
consistent with ``currency does not help'' and ``OpenStreetMap had not yet recorded the
change''; separating them needs edit-history analysis that was not done.}

The claim of this letter: plausibility pressure degrades generated reference imagery for
geometric matching. The adversarial term and a perceptual (LPIPS) reconstruction loss are
each such a pressure, established separately. Where the conditioning input carries no
information, a loss that rewards plausibility causes the generator to invent structure. An
invented edge is a false control point, and for georeferencing a false control point is
worse than no control point, because it displaces the solution silently.

The theory is the perception--distortion trade-off \cite{blau2018perception}, which we
do not contradict: we identify a consumer that sits at the distortion end. Liu, Zhang and
Xiong \cite{liu2019classification} extend the trade-off to a downstream task, but a
semantic one with an error rate, where a hallucinated texture that stays in class costs
nothing; in a geometric task an invented edge is a confident, well-localised observation
of something that does not exist. Two prior loss studies are scored against a positional metric, and both train
translation and matching jointly. Arar \emph{et al.}\ \cite{arar2020unsupervised}
ablate loss terms against registration error and find the opposite sign to ours, because
there the adversarial term does representation-alignment work inside the loop; Ma
\emph{et al.}\ \cite{ma2024visible} add matching losses to an adversarial baseline and
score template-matching accuracy and point end-point error. Neither removes the
adversarial term, and in both the generated image is an intermediate inside the training
loop; here it is a frozen deliverable and the matcher is exogenous. Cohen, Luck and
Honari \cite{cohen2018distribution} showed in medical translation that distribution-matching
losses hallucinate features, and Cohen, Kligvasser \emph{et al.}\ \cite{cohen2024looks}
prove that pursuing perceptual quality converts uncertainty into confidently rendered
false detail, which is our mechanism, predicted. Fuentes Reyes \emph{et al.}\
\cite{fuentesreyes2019sar} named the phenomenon ``fiction'' in SAR-to-optical translation
in 2019 and wrote that no suitable metric existed to evaluate it; Merkle \emph{et al.}\
\cite{merkle2018exploring} established feeding translation output into a matching
pipeline, with matching yield as the objective and never as a diagnostic, and a recent
optical--SAR registration method avoids the translation stage altogether because of the
hallucinated textures it risks \cite{yu2026offsetnet}, without measuring them. Prior work
measures how accurately matches on generated images localise; to our knowledge, none has
asked whether the structure being matched was present in the input.

The contributions, each to our knowledge new: (i) a $2\times2$ factorial that isolates
plausibility pressure with a positional outcome, replicated at seed level; (ii) a cheap,
input-conditioned, reproducible measurement of invention, tied to matchability rather than
to perception; (iii) a design rule for anyone generating reference imagery for a geometric
consumer.
