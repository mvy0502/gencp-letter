Sign convention, used throughout this letter: $\Delta$ = candidate $-$ baseline, so
a negative $\Delta$ means the candidate is better. Residuals are positional errors in
pixels at 10~m ground sampling; lower is better.

\subsection{Model and fine-tuning}

We fine-tune a published conditional GAN \cite{guasch2026gencp} that renders a Sentinel-2-like image from
OpenStreetMap vectors and land-cover raster: a U-Net-256 generator (54.414~M parameters)
with a PatchGAN discriminator, trained image-to-image in the direction from map to image.
Training used 5,577 Turkish image pairs and 20 epochs per arm, under the generator's own
linear schedule---ten epochs at the base learning rate followed by ten decaying to zero.

The factorial was trained in full at six independent seeds (45--50) on one platform, one
A10G GPU per run; that block is the confirmatory evidence behind every seed-level statement
in Section III. It was preceded by the run that generated the hypothesis (seed 42) and by a
two-seed block (seeds 43 and 44), both on a different platform, one T4 GPU per run. Before
the six-seed block was launched, seed 43 was re-trained on the first platform and the two
platforms were compared under a poolability rule registered in advance. The rule returned
\emph{not pooled}: one of eleven registered quantities, the edge ratio of the adversarial
+ L1 arm, differed across platforms by more than its seed-to-seed spread. The two platforms
are therefore analysed as separate blocks throughout, seed counts are reported per block,
and no pooled statistic appears anywhere in this letter. The six-seed block carries the
inference; the two-seed block is reported for consistency only.

The arms carrying an adversarial term also carry a two-epoch warm-up at $2\times10^{-5}$
before eighteen main epochs at $10^{-4}$; the arms without one run twenty main epochs at
$10^{-4}$. Integrated over training this gives the adversarial arms 13.40 against
15.00 in units of $10^{-4}$ epochs, a 10.67\% deficit, borne by the arms that score
worse. The warm-up exists because joint training from a randomly initialised
discriminator at full learning rate is unstable, so the asymmetry is a property of the
design rather than an accident of it.

Its consequence is measured rather than assumed negligible. Giving a
non-adversarial arm that exact schedule moves its positional residual by
$+0.007 \pm 0.034$~px, roughly 1\% of the 0.647~px gap the schedule would have to explain
(one seed, chip-level bound). The LPIPS-only versus L1-only contrast is immune to
it by construction, both arms being un-warmed twenty-epoch arms at identical integrated
learning rate.

\subsection{The design}

A $2\times2$ factorial crosses an adversarial term with the reconstruction loss:
adversarial + L1, L1 alone, adversarial + LPIPS, and LPIPS alone. Held fixed across
all four cells within each replicate: training data, seed, initialisation, evaluation
chips, matcher, and matcher configuration; the seed varies across the six replicates and
never within one. The learning-rate schedule is an explicit exception to that
list, quantified in II-A, not an omission from it.

The published pretrained weights already occupy the adversarial-plus-LPIPS cell, trained
on European data rather than fine-tuned on ours; they are reported as a fifth arm rather
than presented as an empty cell. Our adversarial-plus-LPIPS and LPIPS-only arms reproduce
the repository's executable definition of the published objective---adversarial
+ $\lambda\cdot$LPIPS with $\lambda = 100$ and a binary cross-entropy
discriminator---since the published text does not name the LPIPS backbone and the released
code uses VGG.

\subsection{Disclosure}

The discriminator is not published; only the generator is deposited. Every arm with an
adversarial term therefore begins from a randomly initialised, seeded discriminator,
recorded in a provenance file per run. Section~IV-B shows, on a single-seed, chip-level checkpoint sweep, that this is not
what causes the adversarial arms to lose, but it is a deviation from the published
training setup and we state it as one.

\subsection{Evaluation}

Residuals are KLT feature matches \cite{saunier2024karios} against real Sentinel-2, at a confidence threshold of
0.8, over 130 Ankara chips, each a $228\times228$-pixel window at 10~m, stratified into five
quintiles by land-cover information density. Every number states its inference path and input provenance, and band handling is part
of it: the residuals of Table~I and of Section~III-L are KLT run on the three-band warped
rasters; the invention measurement of II-E and Fig.~2 use a BT.601 gray conversion; the
production-path figure of Section~V-A is KLT on the BT.601 gray band. Test-time dropout is
active---it is the generator's own design, not a defect---and a deterministic mode was
measured to be score-neutral within $\pm0.15$~px on 30 chips and the earlier arms; the
bound was not re-measured for the LPIPS arms.

Each arm's chip residual is a median over the matches that arm itself produced,
and the arms do not produce equally many: median surviving points per chip, averaged over the six seeds, are 51 for the
pretrained generator, 62 for adversarial + L1, 60 for adversarial + LPIPS, 75 for L1 alone
and 87 for LPIPS alone (Table~I). This is selection
on a post-treatment variable and we name it as such.

We test it by equalising counts. On each chip every arm is truncated to the best $K$ of its own matches, ranked by match score (the matcher's own
per-match quality value, descending), where $K$ is the smallest count any arm achieved on
that chip; the LPIPS-only arm surrenders 38\% of its points and the adversarial-plus-LPIPS
arm 7\%. A minimum-match-count sweep over all four arms jointly is reported alongside it.
Both are reported in Section III.

Point-level common support is not constructible here, and we report that rather
than approximating it. KLT keypoints are detected independently per arm on that arm's own
generated image, so the arms share no point identity: at a two-pixel tolerance, 69 of 130
chips have no common points at all (counted at one seed before any contrast was computed), and a tolerance wide enough to yield a usable set would
exceed the 1.4--2.0~px residuals under test and absorb the signal. Equal-count truncation
is therefore the only available substitute. It is ranked by match score and never by
residual, which would be circular; match score is itself post-treatment, so this
removes the count asymmetry rather than all conditioning, and no result here is unbiased.

Inference is at the seed level. Let $m_{a,s,i}$ be arm $a$'s per-chip residual, the
median over its own matches, on chip $i$ in seed $s$, with $N = 130$ chips and $S = 6$
seeds. A contrast between arms $a$ and $b$ is one number per seed, the mean of the
per-chip paired differences, and its interval is taken across seeds:
\begin{subequations}\label{eq:seed}
\begin{align}
\Delta_s &= \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\bigl(m_{a,s,i} - m_{b,s,i}\bigr), \label{eq:seedstat}\\
\mathrm{CI}_{95} &= \bar{\Delta} \pm t_{0.975,\,S-1}\,\frac{\hat{\sigma}_\Delta}{\sqrt{S}},
\quad \hat{\sigma}_\Delta^2 = \frac{1}{S-1}\sum_{s=1}^{S}\bigl(\Delta_s - \bar{\Delta}\bigr)^2. \label{eq:seedci}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
The registered reading of a contrast is the sign of $\Delta_s$ in every seed, with
$P = 2^{-S} = 1/64$ under a null of no effect and the direction fixed in advance; the
interval of (\ref{eq:seedci}) is reported beside it, not required by it. The treatment was
applied once per training run, so a standard error taken across the $N$ chips within one
seed measures how consistently one checkpoint beats another, not how consistently the
intervention works.

\subsection{The invention measurement}

Let $I$ be the input render, warped to the chip grid and converted to 8-bit BT.601 gray,
$Y$ the real chip and $\hat{Y}_a$ arm $a$'s output on the same grid and gray scale, and
$G(X) = \sqrt{S_x^2 + S_y^2}$ the gradient magnitude of $X$ under the $3\times3$ Sobel
kernels without normalisation. The input-silent set of a chip is
$\Omega = \{p : G(I)(p) \le \tau\}$ with $\tau = 20$ on the 8-bit scale, and the chip's
edge ratio for arm $a$ is
\begin{equation}
r_a = \frac{\sum_{p\in\Omega}\,[\,G(\hat{Y}_a)(p) > \tau\,]}{\sum_{p\in\Omega}\,[\,G(Y)(p) > \tau\,]},
\label{eq:edge}
\end{equation}
the arm's edge fraction on $\Omega$ over the real chip's edge fraction on the same
$\Omega$; a chip with an empty $\Omega$ or no real edge on it is excluded, which happens
on none of the 130. Table~\ref{tab:panel} reports the mean of $r_a$ over chips, then over
seeds. The set $\Omega$ is defined by the input, not by the ground truth, deliberately: it separates
\emph{invented where nothing was known} from \emph{wrong where something was known}---a distinction that, to our knowledge, existing hallucination measures do not draw; the
nearest, which decouples a restored image's learned-prior component from its data-fidelity
component \cite{elhelou2022bigprior}, conditions on the prior rather than on the input's
silent regions.

\subsection{Geometry}

Resampling $257\times257$ rasters at 10~m to $256\times256$ while copying the transform
unchanged gives a true ground sampling distance of 10.0390625~m against a declared 10.0~m,
an error of exactly $1/256$, zero at the north-west corner and reaching 14.1~m at the
south-east corner of a chip. Our path corrects it, and the correction is applied to every
chip scored here, so our geometry is not identical to the published pipeline's. The
qualifier travels with the claim: the systematic component is consistent with the signs
and magnitudes of the published means, but the predicted standard deviation is 2.89~m
against an observed 14.5 to 17.3~m, so it accounts for roughly 3.9\% of the reported
variance and does not invalidate the published conclusions. We describe it as a
text-versus-data inconsistency, not as a code bug, and pin the audited upstream commit
(\texttt{e218f29}).
